Why the 18.7% headline was withdrawn

The first C03 result had two living-soffit lights physically overlapping. Their anchors were only 6 cm apart. Recalculating the saved light arrays reproduced the number, but the arrangement cannot be accepted as buildable. C02 also placed four overhead anchors outside the actual mounting slabs. Both results were rejected and sent back for a bounded repair.

What C01 changes

C01 re-aims four overheads at the existing anchors. It retains five overheads (3 ×120 W +2 ×80 W), six standard Targetti heads, no Maxis, and 719.2 W. Pole positions and aims remain fixed. Canopy coverage and the weakest-pad measure improve, while peak electric PPFD falls. Courtyard eye-level spill rises from 3,241 to 3,640 lux, within the inherited 3,666 lux comparison ceiling; that ceiling is not a comfort certification.

Typical-day mean is 4.671 for R06 and 4.965 for C01. Those values include more modelled daylight and must not be compared with dull-day means. No new hardware is needed for C01; re-aiming and commissioning labor is unquoted.

What remains provisional

The additional review checks saved source totals, actual underside anchor footprints and all fixture-body pairs. It does not certify screw patterns, brackets, hinges, supports, waterproofing or installation. Philips 120 W output uses scaled 80 W photometry; shared daylight assumptions, lack of fixture self-shadow and field PAR/thermal verification remain relevant. A 7% DLI gain does not mean 7% more lifespan.

These are best results within the tested candidate sets, not proof of a global optimum. Rejected versions are archived locally. R06 and the shared comparison physics remain unchanged.
